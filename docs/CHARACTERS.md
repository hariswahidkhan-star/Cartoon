# CHARACTERS — *The Lantern Sea*

Great animated films live or die on characters we'd follow anywhere. These two must feel
real enough to love in the first ten minutes.

---

## REN — *the lighthouse keeper*

**Age:** 17 · **Role:** Protagonist / POV

**Design notes (anime):** lean, slightly too-tall-for-his-clothes, perpetual sea-wind in
his dark hair. A keeper's oil-stained coat too big for him (it was his mother's). Warm
amber eyes — the color of lantern-light, a quiet visual rhyme. One hand always a little
sooty from trimming wicks. His silhouette should read instantly: boy + lamp.

**Want (what he thinks he needs):** to hear his mother's voice one last time.
**Need (what the story gives him):** to stop living among the dead and let himself love the
living — and then be brave enough to let go.

**Flaw:** he holds on. To grief, to the lamp, to Suzu. His love is real but it wants to
*keep*. The film cures him of this, gently and at terrible cost.

**Voice / feel:** dry, self-deprecating, surprisingly funny once he warms up. Talks to the
lanterns like old friends because they're the only company he's had.

**Arc in one line:** *the boy who searched the sky for a voice becomes the man who gives
someone the courage to become one.*

---

## SUZU — *the girl who fell*

**Age:** appears 16 · **Role:** Deuteragonist / the heart

**Design notes (anime):** faintly luminous, as if lit from inside — skin, hair, and eyes
all hold a soft glow that brightens with emotion (our key magical "tell"). Pale silver-gold
hair that drifts slightly, as if underwater, even in still air. Barefoot. Her clothes look
woven from light. As the film goes on and she becomes more human — more *alive* — the glow
paradoxically softens, because she's choosing the warm, mortal thing over the cosmic one.

**Want:** to know who she is and why she's pulled upward.
**Need:** to choose — freely, knowingly — to be the light, because she was first allowed to
be a person.

**The tragedy of her design:** every wonderful human thing she learns (taste, rain,
laughter, Ren) is a reason *not* to ascend. The audience falls in love with her exactly as
fast as the cost of losing her climbs.

**Voice / feel:** curious about everything, no social filter at first (she's new to being a
person), then quick-witted and radiant. Her laugh is the sound the whole film is built to
protect.

**Arc in one line:** *a light that becomes a girl, so that she can choose to become a
dawn.*

---

## THE WARDEN OF THE CROWN — *the truth, not the villain*

**Role:** The keeper of the cycle at the top of the world. Ancient, serene, sorrowful. Has
watched a thousand Dawns rise and knows the price of each.

**Design notes:** faceless warm light in the shape of a robed figure; voice like many
voices at once (every past Dawn). Not cruel — the Warden grieves with them. The film has
**no traditional antagonist**; the "enemy" is *time, death, and love's refusal to let go.*
This is a mature, *Lion King*-grade choice — the conflict is inside the heart.

---

## Supporting cast (develop in script)

- **MOTHER (lantern)** — Ren's mother, heard only as a memory-voice. The emotional anchor
  of his backstory and the key to the ending.
- **THE ISLE-FOLK** — small communities on each tier, each a flavor of a dimming world:
  some clinging to hope, some already giving up. They show the stakes without exposition.
- **THE FIRST FALLEN LANTERN** — a child's lantern Ren fails to save early on. Small, brutal,
  sets the clock emotionally.

---

## Relationship dynamics

- **Ren ↔ Suzu:** opposites who complete. He knows death; she's pure life. He listens; she
  speaks. He's spent too long still; she can't stop moving upward. Their chemistry is the
  film. Protect it in every scene.
- **Ren ↔ the lanterns:** his first family. His ability to hear them is the plot *and* the
  theme — the whole movie is about what the dead would want for the living.
